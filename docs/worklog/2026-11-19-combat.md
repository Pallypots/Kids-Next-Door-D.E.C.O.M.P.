For the integrator: this holds what belongs in docs/STATE.md, docs/verification.md and docs/portability.md. Subsystem `gm/combat` (no other subsystem is a prefix of it, nor it of another). State of this work: Semantic-tested. A host MATCH from `tools/verify.py` says nothing about the original compiler.

## The branch this sits on

Reset to `worktree-agent-a5e6dc2b9bb379207` (ea83e5d, the plsetup work), as asked. Like that branch it carries integrate-batch4 underneath, with gmplayer and plsetup on top; the gmnpc branch (e069650) is **not** under it.

## What a real build does with this

Nothing reaches it from data yet. Called directly, the combat update now runs its own code completely: it spawns the weapon sets' actors once, finds the threat among the enemies (by priority, then distance, ThreatByRadius's ranges or the sight cone), keeps the attack range the threat is in with its hysteresis and sends THREAT, UNTHREAT, THREATAC and the ranges' enter events; sends TARGETME and TARGETNO; keeps, drops and chooses the lock-on target (LOCKON? to each candidate, by angle, distance and the aim cone, with priorities), sends HAVETRGT and LOSTTRGT; counts the ZapLights in reach (ZAPSTART, ZAPEND); turns the sticks into the tags LOCKL_DN/LOCKR_DN, TAGL_DN/TAGR_DN and the bump tags; and hands the target's note bank to the display. Where it stops, in the order a real fight meets them:

* Its first test is the owner's node slot 27 called directly (0x0014C170, gmactor's seam, 62 bytes): a real build stops there on every frame. `test_combat --seam` checks it stops. This is the first thing to recover after this.
* Every enemy search goes through the dynamic node iterator's Find and Next (0x00164760, 0x001646C0), seams.
* The sight test (0x00130E50, 58 functions beneath it), the player's stick and its angle (0x00093300, 0x00093350), the attached PCNPC (0x00169730), the aim node (0x0014C300), the spawner (0x00153CA0) and the input's button (0x0008F5D0) are seams.
* The weapon sets' value is found through FindComponent, whose registry search (0x000679E0) is still a seam on this branch.
* On this branch nothing sets FaHud::s_instance (0x003B353C), GmSpawnManager::s_instance (0x003B3A3C), GmDynamicNodeLoader::s_instance or App: a real build would crash reading them.

The NPCs' combat user from 0x00092060 is FaAcNPC's slot 15 (0x00091FE0, 221 bytes): it makes a FaCmCombat when the record says Combat, attaches it and calls its slot 3. It is already recovered on the gmnpc branch, where FaCmCombat's constructor and slots are seams; at integration those seams go, and the plsetup classes and this update stand in their place. Nothing of it was redone here.

Findings:

* **FaCmCombat's words, from the update.** +0x2C and +0x30 are values used instead of ThreatEnterRange and ThreatExitRange, +0x78 and +0x7C lists used instead of Enemies and EnemyPriorities (nothing here sets them); +0x94 there is a threat, +0x98 its attack range, +0x9C the ranges' events are sent, +0xA0 others may take this owner for a threat (read on targets' modules), +0xA4 the threat's priority; +0xA8 an enemy's answer to LOCKON? (its script writes it), +0xAC TARGETME and the lock-on's choosing without the PCNPC; +0xB8, +0xC0, +0xC8 the digital bump, analog bump and lock tags (eight-byte names); +0xD0 the lock tag held back; +0xD4 the ZapLights in reach; +0xD8 the target's name to the display; +0xDC and +0xE0 HAVETRGT and LOSTTRGT sent. GmCmCombat's +0x08 is "weapons to spawn": the update spawns them and clears it, slot 5 and Stop set it. Commented in the header; the names are kept, being plsetup's.
* **The weapons are actors**: SpawnWeapons spawns each place's name through 0x00153CA0 and calls the actor's slots 4 and 32 and sends SPAWN. GmCmWeapon's slot 4 (the release) is the actor's slot 4.
* **The attack ranges**: a range's value's descriptor's real floor (+0x20) and ceiling (+0x24) are its bounds; the threat stays in last frame's range while within its ceiling, then the last later range whose floor it is within wins.
* **AtVector3::Distance (0x00042F60) returns its result unrounded in the x87 register**; three places here compare it before storing (the ZapLight radius, InRange's horizontal distance, ChooseTarget's nearest). The oracle showed it (an actor visited twice was taken the second time by the original). The recovered Distance returns float; this work uses a local `DistanceInRegister` where the original compares. **Request**: Distance should return double, as the original does; other callers that compare it unstored may be wrong today without their oracles showing it.
* AngleTo (0x00092C50) returns acos unrounded too, and InAimCone compares its vertical angle so.
* 0x00013E50 is a case-blind compare of n characters with its own register convention (names in ecx and eax), using the C library's **tolower** (0x00125DCF); it returns the difference of the characters as they are. 0x00013F50 is it with 8.
* "Under control", for the update, is a character with a controller and without the player flag 0x100 in m_flags98.
* 0x00154540 (folded, AtAbstractController::AxisCount) is GmAcCharController::Owner, already inline in the header.

## Oracle

No loader reaches this code, so the cases are combat objects built by each side's own constructor (FaCmCombat 0x000A3360 or FaCmPlayerCombat 0x000A4D00, and placement new) and called directly, the original running its own code at its addresses: each function on its own over states, and the update over runs of frames that carry the state from one frame to the next.

**What stands in, on both sides alike, and so is not tested:** the actors (plain blocks laid out as characters, each side its own copy of one world, with one fake vftable whose slots 4, 6, 22, 24, 27, 32, 42, 49, 53, 55 and 59 log and answer from the case; LOCKON? writes the asker's answer word as a script would), their scene and aim nodes (slot 2 logs and moves the centre), controllers, stats, the record's values, enemy lists, priorities, attack ranges and weapon sets (plain data); every seam above and the node's slot 27, one double each for both sides (the iterator walks a list of actors by name from the case, null items included). Compared: return values (reals by their bits, AngleTo's rounded to float), every word of the combat object, of each actor (their target words above all), of the weapon places, nodes and aim nodes and the display's name, the count spawned, and the log of every call out with its arguments (event names by content, the events' words with their vftables paired by class, distances passed to the sight test by their bits).

## Quirks kept

* The display's pointer is read before anything else in the update. The lock tag is held back for one frame after a frame not under control.
* SpawnWeapons tests each set's address for null, clears an empty place again before reading its name, and passes an empty name by.
* UpdateThreat uses priorities only when EnemyPriorities itself is there, though it reads the override at +0x7C in its place; it copies the owner's +0x04's name and does not use it; it does not test the iterator's Begin. A threat settled by the current target is not searched past unless priorities are in use.
* UpdateTargetMe calls the owner's slot 42 before testing the owner for null.
* UpdateLockOn reads the controller without testing it, and writes the target word only when it differs.
* UpdateZap tests a null item's actor through the node's slot 27 (a crash); ZAPEND is tested as unsigned.
* ReadLockStick writes the tag as eight bytes, unterminated; rounds the stick's squared magnitude to float before its root; its bands are closed below and open above.
* TestThreat replaces the owner's position by the start position (slot 24) for the distance, rounds the distance to float, hands the sight test a pointer to it and uses what it leaves there.
* IsAlive answers 1 for anything that is not an enabled character with stats, null included.
* PostThreat sends its event (naming the threat) with THREAT and the range's enter event only; a range of -1 sends nothing.
* TargetPosition, InRange and InAimCone, given no target, use the owner's slot 53 word only to give up when it is 0; TargetPosition then asks for the aim node of null.
* IsEnemy compares type names as the original's two unterminated eight-byte copies (an eight-character name reads into the next); siblings are the owner's +0x04 type name and the same slot 6 name.
* ChooseTarget lets an actor through, by side 0, when its distance is less than the best distance or than the best cone *angle*; distance is FLT_MAX for the nearest unless side is 0; side 1 and 2 measure the angle the other way round by the sign of a cross product.
* AtCompareNoCaseN returns the difference of the characters as they are, not lowered.

## Not compared, and why

* IsEnemy's type names of eight characters: the original's strcmp then reads past its second buffer into its own stack; the cases keep that comparison inside the two buffers (they differ, or one is shorter).
* Characters above 0x7F in AtCompareNoCaseN: the original's tolower indexes its table by the signed character (before the table); the texts have none.
* x87 against SSE: the oracle runs both at the host's 53-bit precision. acos and AngleTo's result: the image's acos against the host's, compared rounded to float; an exact-boundary vertical angle could differ in its last bit.

## Portability

* GmAcActor +0x17C (slot 53) and +0x04 hold pointers in 32-bit words, as gmnpc noted; the recovered code casts through them as the original does. FaCmCombat's +0x2C, +0x30, +0x78 and +0x7C are pointers kept in `int` members (plsetup's types). On a 64-bit host they cannot hold a pointer; they should be retyped. The host test only uses them null.
* Slot 49's second argument carries a pointer to the event in an `int`, slot 24's first a pointer to a vector: the same.
